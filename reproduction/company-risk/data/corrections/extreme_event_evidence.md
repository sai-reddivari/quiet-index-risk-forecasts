# Extreme-event evidence

This file records the evidence behind every row of `extreme_event_ledger_2026-09-30.csv`. The ledger covers every daily simple total return beyond plus or minus 50% in the cleaned adjusted close of the 503 snapshot securities through the research cutoff of 30 September 2026 (docs/STUDY_SPEC.md section 3). With corrections switched off the loader finds exactly 13 such returns, and no further one occurs between 1 and 12 October 2026 in the frozen vintage.

Each event was reviewed on 13 October 2026 by one reviewer and checked by two or three independent verifiers, who were asked to refute the entry. The reviewers and verifiers read the frozen Yahoo pull through 2026-10-12 (never the 2026-10-13 leftovers), Financial Modeling Prep end-of-day prices, splits and dividends pulled for the purpose, SEC EDGAR filings, and contemporaneous press. The decisions were fixed from those votes; this file and the ledger were written on 14 October 2026 from them.

Every return quoted as "original" or "retained" below is the value the shared loader computes from the stored floats of the 2026-10-12 vintage with corrections off, at full precision. Several reviewers had computed returns from prices rounded to a few decimals, which differ from the loader's value in the seventh to tenth decimal; the verifiers caught these slips (AMD, CVNA, GL, MRNA and TRGP), and the ledger carries the loader's values instead. Adjacent returns are the loader's returns for the session immediately before and immediately after the event; no event window contains a missing session, so no return bridges a gap. FMP prices are cited only as individual closing prints that the press also reports.

The four classifications and treatments follow the specification: a verified market move is retained unchanged; a verified vendor error gets an evidenced correction applied separately from the raw data; a corporate-action discrepancy has its distribution-inclusive return reconstructed; an unresolved event is quarantined and disclosed. Twelve events are verified market moves and are retained. One, DHR on 5 July 2016, is a corporate-action discrepancy corrected by a back adjustment. Nothing is quarantined.

## DHR 2016-07-05

On 1 June 2016 Danaher declared a pro rata dividend of Fortive common stock, one FTV share for every two DHR shares, record date 15 June 2016, distribution completed at 12:01 a.m. on Saturday 2 July 2016 and credited through DTC on Tuesday 5 July (8-K 0001193125-16-609932, EX-99.1; completion in 8-K 0001193125-16-643649, item 2.01). When-issued FTV and ex-distribution DHR markets ran from 13 June, and regular-way FTV trading began on 5 July, which makes 5 July the first regular-way DHR session without the entitlement. Danaher's Form 8937 values the distribution at $24.56 per DHR share, from NYSE high-low averages on 5 July of $78.94 for DHR and $49.11 for FTV.

Yahoo booked the event twice. Its split-adjusted close carries a 1.319 "split" on 5 July, and its adjusted close additionally carries a cash "dividend" of 24.56, which is the Form 8937 value in as-traded dollars. The adjusted-to-split-adjusted ratio is flat everywhere in DHR's 2015 to 2026 history except on 5 July 2016, where it jumps by a factor of 1.5555146, matching 1/(1 - 24.56/68.7712) = 1.5555153; the next largest relative change anywhere in the history is 0.0021. The downloaded adjusted return is therefore +61.22% and counts the distribution twice, while the split-adjusted close return of +3.64% is also wrong because the vendor's 1.319 ratio does not equal the realised value ratio on the ex date. FMP repeats the same treatment (a 1319/1000 split and a 24.56 dividend on 5 July 2016, and a dividend-adjusted return of +61.3%), so FMP is not an independent check on the adjusted series, although it confirms the split-adjusted prices and volume.

Independent prices: undoing each vendor's own later factors (1.319 and 1.128 for DHR, 1.195 and 1.327 for FTV) reconstructs as-traded closes of 102.32 for DHR on 1 July, 80.40 for DHR on 5 July and 48.60 for FTV's first regular-way close on 5 July. The Everett Herald reported FTV's first close at $48.60, Danaher's FY2016 10-K quarterly ranges (102.79 to 92.45 in Q2, 82.64 to 76.15 in Q3) fit both DHR levels, and FMP's split-adjusted DHR closes (68.74 and 71.27) give 102.27 and 80.39 as traded. DHR volume on 5 July was 17.6 million on the vendors' current share basis, against 6.2 million on 1 July, consistent with spin-related trading.

The correction follows the reviewer's derivation, which all three verifiers accepted. The distribution-inclusive gross return over the ex date is (80.40 + 0.5 x 48.60) / 102.32 = 1.0232603597, valuing the half FTV share at FTV's first regular-way close. Every DHR adjusted close strictly before 5 July 2016 is multiplied by the factor that makes the ex-date return equal that value: factor = adj(5 July) / (adj(1 July) x 1.0232603597) = 67.60747528076172 / (41.93535232543945 x 1.0232603597) = 1.5755357848732043, which agrees with the reviewer's 1.575535785 to ten significant figures. The corrected return is 0.023260359655981278 (ledger) and the loader reproduces it as 0.023260359655981055. Because one constant scales every earlier price, every daily return before the event is unchanged apart from floating-point rounding in the last binary digit; multi-day measures that span the date, such as volatility over a window containing 5 July 2016, do change.

Adjacent returns: 1 July 2016 is +0.013069424706631638 (+1.3069%, matched by FMP's 67.85 to 68.74) and 6 July is +0.0027366862319437857 (+0.2737%, matched by FMP's 71.27 to 71.46). The adjustment ratio is flat across both, so neither is affected, and 4 July was a market holiday, so the three sessions are consecutive.

Verifier outcome: three of three accepted the classification, the treatment and the numbers. Their caveats are recorded here because they bound the correction. First, the factor depends on rounding the reconstructed as-traded closes to cents (102.32 and 80.40); the unrounded reconstructions (102.3199951 and 80.4000004) give a return of 0.0232604118 and a factor of 1.5755357046, a difference below 1e-7 that changes no decision. Second, valuing the distribution at the FTV close is a convention (the one CRSP uses), not something a source fixes; valuing it at the Form 8937 high-low average of 49.11 gives (80.40 + 24.555)/102.32 - 1 = 0.025753 (the reviewer's 0.024752 for this case was an arithmetic slip), and FMP's DHR closes give 0.023652. The corrected return therefore lies between about 2.33% and 2.58% under any reasonable convention, far from the threshold. Third, the reviewer's decomposition check (1.555515318 x 1.012871113) multiplies to 1.5755366, not 1.575535785; the gap of 5e-7 comes from Yahoo's stored adjusted closes, and the factor used is the directly computed one. Fourth, FMP's FTV intraday high on 5 July (about 54.34 as traded) is inconsistent with the Form 8937 average and should not be cited; the FTV close is confirmed independently.

Treatment: corporate_action_discrepancy, back_adjust_before_date with factor 1.5755357848732043 and corrected return 0.023260359655981278. Original return 0.6121833138802235; corrected return 0.023260359655981055 as the loader computes it.

Notes on Danaher's other distributions, which are not ledger events because neither return comes near plus or minus 50%. Envista left Danaher on 18 December 2019 through a split-off exchange offer, not a pro rata distribution (8-K 0000313616-19-000174: 22,921,984 DHR shares accepted in exchange for 127,868,000 NVST shares). Holders who did not tender kept unchanged DHR shares, so no price adjustment is warranted; neither vendor books one, the adjustment ratio is flat through December 2019, and the 18 December 2019 return is -0.50%. Veralto was spun off as one VLTO share per three DHR shares, ex date 2 October 2023 (8-K 0000313616-23-000252). Both vendors book it only as a 1.128 "split", the when-issued ratio, with no double count, and the downloaded return that day is -2.822%. The distribution-inclusive return valued at VLTO's first regular-way close of 85.12 is (213.74 + 85.12/3)/248.10 - 1 = -2.413%, so the vendor data understate that day by 0.41 percentage points. That gap is recorded here and not treated.

## OXY 2020-03-09

Occidental Petroleum, carrying heavy debt from the Anadarko acquisition, closed at 12.51 on 9 March 2020 against 26.86 on 6 March, a close-to-close fall of 53.4%, on 104.9 million shares. It opened at 15.58, traded as high as 19.19 and as low as 12.04, and the Motley Fool reported it down 53% at the close as oil had its worst day since 1991. The same day was the ex date of the ordinary quarterly dividend of $0.79 (declared 11 February, record 10 March, paid 15 April); the Q1 2020 10-Q (accession 0000797468-20-000010) confirms $0.79 per share was paid and that the cut to $0.11 was announced on 10 March. The 8-Ks from February to March 2020 cover results, a rights plan and the Icahn agreements, with no split, spin-off or special distribution.

The adjusted-to-split-adjusted ratio rises by a factor of 1.0303034 on 9 March, from 0.9000770 to 0.9273524, and the inverse of that step, 0.9705884, equals 1 - 0.79/26.86 to float32 precision. That is Yahoo's ordinary multiplicative dividend adjustment, applied the same way on every OXY ex date from 2019 to 2020. FMP's dividend-adjusted closes (24.17 and 11.60) use the same convention, and FMP's as-traded closes and volume (26.86, 12.51, 104,930,331) match Yahoo. The return before (6 March) is -0.14676012850273212 and the return after (10 March, the rebound on the dividend and capex cut) is +0.1462828813623136, identical on the adjusted and close series.

The reviewer proposed classifying the event as a corporate-action discrepancy and back-adjusting so that the return would equal the additive, distribution-inclusive figure (12.51 + 0.79)/26.86 - 1 = -0.5048399134, instead of Yahoo's multiplicative -0.5201379100; the arithmetic of that proposal (a factor of 0.9691049480 on prices before 9 March) is correct. Both verifiers rejected it. There is no discrepancy or adjustment break: both vendors apply the standard dividend convention exactly as on every other ex date, and the specification defines returns as daily simple total returns from the cleaned adjusted close, so -0.5201379100 is the study's own return. Correcting one ordinary ex date only because its return crosses the ledger threshold would mix two dividend conventions in one panel; one verifier checked all 17,635 inferred ex-date observations in the pull and found only six where the two conventions differ by more than 0.5 percentage points, of which DHR, KDP, BKR and GEN are genuine special distributions and OXY is the only ordinary dividend. Whether to adopt the additive convention panel-wide is a design question for Sai, not a ledger correction. The back adjustment would also have changed every multi-day measure spanning 9 March 2020.

Decision: verified_market_move, retain, as both verifiers recommended. Original and retained return -0.5201379099996899. The return stays beyond -50% under either convention.
